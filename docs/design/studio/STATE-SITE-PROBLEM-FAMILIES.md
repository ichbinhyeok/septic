# State site-problem families

## Scope

Three high-intent state guide families now have distinct studio presentations:

- Failed perc test: 10 evidence-ready state pages.
- Septic replacement area: 10 evidence-ready state pages.
- Wet yard over a septic drain field: 10 evidence-ready state pages.

States without a published source page are not fabricated to complete a fifty-state set.

## Visual theses

- Failed perc: a calm soil-review report that turns a negative result into a sequence of questions rather than a verdict.
- Replacement area: a parcel-planning folio where the recorded drawing and the open ground carry the story.
- Wet yard: a restrained field-triage guide that treats visible symptoms seriously without presenting them as a diagnosis.

## Content plans

### Failed perc

1. Full-bleed parcel hero.
2. Explain what the result does and does not establish.
3. Separate ground, footprint, and acceptance path.
4. Show the plan, investigation method, local decision route, sources, and next action.

### Replacement area

1. Full-bleed property hero.
2. Reframe open yard as constrained future infrastructure.
3. Reconcile existing system, protected area, and current parcel.
4. Show redesign pressure, local acceptance, sources, and inquiry path.

### Wet yard

1. Full-bleed symptom-led hero.
2. Separate visible evidence from diagnosis.
3. Move through observe, protect, and diagnose.
4. Connect the symptom to records, local routes, official sources, and inquiry.

## Interaction thesis

- A restrained hero entrance establishes urgency without alarmism.
- Sequential rows shift on hover to reinforce order of operations.
- Local routes, sources, and FAQs disclose progressively rather than becoming a wall of cards.

## Boundaries

All property, plan, and workspace imagery is illustrative. A repeated image never claims to depict the named state, county, or customer property. The pages retain the original state-specific official sources, FAQs, workflow evidence, and internal links.
