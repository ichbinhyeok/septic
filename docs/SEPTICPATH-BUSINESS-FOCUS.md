# SepticPath business focus

Status: working decision, 2026-09-21
Branch: `codex/premium-visual-redesign`

## The decision

SepticPath is not a general septic-content business and it is not a calculator collection. It is an address-based record-research service with SEO distribution.

The existing commercial transaction is already defined:

1. A customer submits a property question.
2. SepticPath researches public sources and contacts the correct agency when needed, free of charge.
3. If useful property-matched evidence can answer the question, the customer receives a free evidence preview showing source, scope, match, answerable questions and limits.
4. The customer may unlock the located originals and human-reviewed property-specific explanation for US $29.
5. Approved agency fees are separate and charged only at cost.

No new product definition is required before this model is tested with real offers and payments.

## What the evidence says

- Search and GA4 growth is real, but demand is concentrated in record-finder, agency, permit and jurisdiction-specific pages.
- In the complete 2026-09-04 through 2026-09-17 window, Organic Search produced 863 sessions and 20 of 21 measured record-help submissions.
- The five largest organic landing pages produced 300 sessions, while the number of organic landing pages was flat at 206 versus 207 in the previous period.
- Calculators and broad reading content receive little meaningful use. Their post-use conversion is therefore not yet the primary problem; discovery and differentiated demand are.
- There have been inquiries but no payment yet. The current evidence proves problem interest, not willingness to pay.

## Product hierarchy

### Product

The free research, evidence preview and optional US $29 reviewed record package.

### Distribution

Agency-, state- and county-specific record/permit pages that match urgent property questions.

### Proof

Anonymized cases showing the question, search obstacle, agency route, returned evidence, answer and limits.

### Support assets

Guides and tools that help a customer understand or prepare for the record workflow. They are not independent product lines.

### Experiments

Broad calculators and editorial families. Keep them stable while they are measured, but do not give them equal navigation weight or add more pages without evidence of discovery or business contribution.

## The next milestone

The primary growth milestone is acquisition, not checkout optimization:

> Grow complete-window organic entrances by expanding the search surface around proven record/permit intent, while preserving every currently performing URL.

The latest complete 28-day GSC comparison moved from 529 to 623 clicks (+17.8%), but impressions moved only from 22,881 to 23,330 (+2.0%). The immediate constraint is therefore reach: SepticPath is harvesting existing visibility slightly better, but it has not yet opened much more search demand.

Paid behavior remains a secondary validation metric. Present the existing evidence-preview offer to every eligible inquiry, but do not substitute checkout work for acquisition work.

## Operating loop

1. Capture the exact customer question and deadline.
2. Research the property, jurisdiction and custodian using the existing operations workflow.
3. Close the documentary question or state the remaining limit.
4. For an eligible result, create the existing preview and US $29 unlock offer promptly.
5. Record offer viewed, checkout started, purchase completed or declined, and fulfillment outcome.
6. Turn reusable route knowledge into the relevant jurisdiction page.
7. Turn privacy-reviewed completed work into proof, not generic content.

This produces the moat:

`search demand → real inquiry → agency route knowledge → verified result → paid package → anonymized proof → stronger high-intent page`

## Portfolio rules

Classify every page family using GSC and GA4, not aesthetic preference:

- **Scale:** qualified traffic, engagement or inquiries exist.
- **Repair:** impressions or near-page-one rankings exist, but clicks or usage are weak.
- **Support:** low acquisition value, but materially helps customers or operations.
- **Hold:** no meaningful indexing, impressions, use or business contribution. Make no additional investment while preserving URLs until an SEO-safe disposition is decided.

Do not mass-delete or redirect the Hold group during the visual redesign. URL consolidation and redesign should be separate changes so ranking movement remains attributable and recoverable.

## What not to do now

- Do not create more broad calculator or reading families.
- Do not keep redesigning the already-defined paid package before customers see it.
- Do not treat every existing page as an equal service.
- Do not measure the premium redesign before it is deployed; the current GA4 baseline describes the old live experience.
- Do not infer willingness to pay from inquiry submissions.

## Thirty-day acquisition program

### Lane 1: harvest existing visibility

Improve the pages that already have large impression pools without changing their URLs or core intent. Priorities are TDEC, North Carolina records, Indiana records, Texas OSSF, Florida OSTDS and the Alabama cost page. Work from the actual query language already shown in GSC, strengthen the first-screen answer and internal links, and measure page-level clicks, CTR and position on complete windows.

### Lane 2: expand adjacent demand

Create or materially deepen pages only where a winning cluster exposes an adjacent query, jurisdiction or agency route. New pages should inherit real custodian, identifier, request-channel and failure-mode knowledge from operations. Do not create another uniform national family before the first expansion cohort is indexed and measured.

### Lane 3: repair index distribution

Audit crawled-not-indexed and discovered-not-indexed pages by family. Improve or consolidate weak duplication only in a separate, reversible SEO change; do not mix URL removals or redirects into the premium visual deployment.

### Lane 4: run one non-record experiment

Use only the two existing non-record signals as the test bed: Alabama system/perc cost and West Virginia perc-test cost. Correct query-to-page mismatch, strengthen unique state evidence and measure whether impressions and clicks grow. Do not expand the calculator or reading families nationally until this small cohort proves it can earn search distribution.

## Thirty-day decision framework

At the end of the first real offer window:

- If Google impressions expand in the targeted clusters, deepen those clusters before opening a new topic.
- If clicks rise while impressions stay flat, the work improved harvesting but did not expand reach; the next cycle must add adjacent qualified query coverage.
- If edited pages lose position, revert the specific content change rather than changing the whole family.
- If the Alabama and West Virginia experiment grows, apply its proven structure to a small second cohort; otherwise stop broad calculator expansion.
- If customers purchase, improve fulfillment speed and expand the acquisition pages that produce eligible cases.
- If customers view the offer but do not begin checkout, test the preview, promise, price presentation and perceived certainty.
- If they begin checkout but do not pay, investigate payment trust and checkout friction.
- If inquiries rarely produce eligible evidence, improve targeting and route coverage rather than the checkout.
- If broad tools remain unused, leave them as low-priority assets or remove them from primary navigation; do not invest further merely because they already exist.

## Immediate assignment

Finish the premium branch with an SEO-preservation gate, but do not add another broad product family. Then run a measured acquisition cycle on the existing high-impression record pages plus the two-page cost/perc experiment. In operations, continue applying the existing paid-unlock flow to eligible results without making it the acquisition team's main project.
